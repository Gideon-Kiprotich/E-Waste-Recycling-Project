# Bounded standalone-parts intake design

Date: 5 October 2026 (Africa/Nairobi). Change: **CR-PARTS-01**. Branch: `parts-intake-design`, created from clean `main` at `c12fc2cc4c148e6e780ed0a38676996fbd6161aa`.

Status: **design authorized; implementation not started**. This is a documentation increment after closed Phase 2, not Phase 3. The project user authorizes the bounded design without claiming stakeholder interviews, physical trials or real collection-point acceptance. Effective requirements are the preserved D2/D3 v1.0 baselines plus CR-ID-01 plus CR-PARTS-01 (v1.1). The matching appended entries preserve prior history.

## Evidence and design basis

Inspected AGENTS.md, both requirements/change entries, the original parts assessment, Phase 2's final acceptance, the existing registration/observation actions and requests, category/question declarations, identifier implementation, migrations, ownership policy, views and reviewed design guidance. Phase 2's completed checks and manual acceptance remain historical evidence for the existing implementation; they do not verify this unimplemented extension.

- **Verified technical evidence:** NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 distinguishes media-appropriate sanitization, verification and validation; degaussing is unsuitable for SSD flash. This supports a storage gate, not a claim that this application performs or certifies sanitization. [NIST, §§3.1.2, 4.5–4.6](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-88r2.pdf).
- **Verified operational guidance:** NEMA discusses segregation by compatibility/destination and recording received waste types/quantities. It does not establish acceptance of these parts at a named Nairobi point. [NEMA e-waste guidance, collection/storage sections](https://nema.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/E-WASTE-GUIDELINES.pdf).
- **Verified reason to retain the exclusion:** lithium-ion batteries can present fire risks, including during transport. This is technical evidence, not Kenyan legal advice or a battery-handling procedure. [US EPA](https://www.epa.gov/recycle/used-lithium-ion-batteries).

These existing authoritative sources were rechecked on 5 October 2026. Category names, fan limits, schema, observation vocabulary and reporting cohorts below are **project design decisions**. Consumer identification, physically affixed labels and competent later inspection are realistic assumptions; software cannot prove physical sameness, electrical safety or an operator's real capabilities. Operational acceptance remains unverified and is not a prerequisite for completing this software design.

## 1. Exact intake vocabulary and eligibility

Keep existing categories `laptop`, `desktop`, `phone`, `flat_monitor`, `printer`, `peripheral`; keep `peripheral` subtypes `router` and `keyboard`. Add exactly one category:

- `standalone_part` — **Standalone ICT part**.
- Its mandatory subtypes are `hdd` — **Hard disk drive (HDD)**, `ssd` — **Solid-state drive (SSD)**, and `ict_cooling_fan` — **Small ICT cooling fan**.

No `other`, battery, RAM, motherboard, power supply, optical drive, USB flash drive, memory card, hybrid/unknown storage type, industrial/mains fan, quantity or bulk vocabulary is added. A drive is one already separate HDD/SSD unit, including its integral housing; an M.2 SSD is a drive, not arbitrary circuit-board intake. A storage enclosure containing several drives or a mixed bag is not one eligible unit. A consumer unable to distinguish HDD from SSD must stop with scope guidance rather than guess or open the unit. Do not instruct removal from a computer or enclosure. Existing intact devices remain registrable through their existing categories.

For this project, a small ICT cooling fan is **one dedicated computer/ICT cooling fan or blower, rated at no more than 24 V DC, with housing/frame no more than 140 mm in its largest dimension (excluding free leads)**. Factory motor-driver, tachometer and PWM circuitry are within this definition. Separate control hubs, networking/storage modules, assembled cooling systems, household ventilation appliances and fans needing mains power are excluded. These numeric bounds are conservative scope choices, not statutory voltage limits or proof of safety. Known suitability can come from an already accessible label/model or existing knowledge; no powered measurement, dismantling or internal inspection is required.

At initial fan registration, the consumer must select `answers.fan_scope=YES` to confirm that definition. `NO` and `UNKNOWN` are visible choices but block registration with: **“This intake is limited to small ICT cooling fans up to 24 V DC and 140 mm. If you are unsure, do not test or dismantle the fan.”** Unknown voltage/dimensions/application cannot be used to admit an arbitrary fan. All ordinary condition questions may still be Unknown.

`answers.fan_electronics=BASIC` means only the integral fan circuitry described above; `UNKNOWN` admits an otherwise positively identified eligible fan with unresolved electronics/data status and review guidance; `ADDITIONAL` means known separate/smart electronics and blocks initial registration with: **“Fans with additional control, network or storage modules are outside this intake scope.”** Do not classify an ambiguous attached controller as an ordinary non-data-bearing fan. No photo classifier or certification is introduced.

## 2. Identity, privacy and origin

Every eligible part uses the existing **one `subjects` row and one `items` row, sharing the same UUID**, immutable `subjects.kind=item`, one opaque EWC code, one initial custody projection and the normal registration event/command. QR/manual lookup, printing and replacement use that subject. Serial-less units rely on that label; absent identifiers are not deduplicated by model, photograph or owner. A consumer must reuse the registered physical unit's identity, not intentionally register it again with an unknown serial; software cannot automatically identify two serial-less submissions as the same object.

CR-ID-01 remains exact: optional serial; trim, remove whitespace and ASCII hyphens, uppercase ASCII letters, preserve leading zeroes; existing encrypted ciphertext, HMAC context/bytes and `app-key-context-1`; partial unique `items_identifier_hmac_unique` on non-null `identifier_hmac`. The namespace stays global across owners, categories, manufacturers, Serial/IMEI, lifecycle states and key-version labels. Empty/separator-only values store null crypto/type fields. No plaintext identifier is copied to logs, events, reports, QR or a storage facet.

Parts accept only `identifier_kind=serial`, or omitted kind with the existing Serial interpretation. Serial input retains the existing 100-raw-character limit and ASCII letters/digits/whitespace/hyphen allowlist; ciphertext retains trimmed input, without changing comparison normalization. Explicit IMEI, including with an empty value, is rejected for a part: **“Use a serial number for a standalone part, or leave it blank if unknown.”** Existing whole-device Serial/IMEI and Luhn validation remain unchanged. A part's numeric serial still conflicts with the equal normalized IMEI of any existing device. Preserve the exact duplicate error: **“This serial/IMEI is already registered. Please recheck the number entered.”** No matched device, owner, code or private link is disclosed.

Add immutable server-owned `items.intake_class`, with first-increment values `whole_device` and `standalone_part`. The server derives the latter only from `standalone_part` category. It asserts standalone consumer intake, not evidence that the object never had a physical parent. Existing categories get `whole_device`; no old row is reclassified as a part. There is **no parent/removal relationship, detachment event or batch membership** for a standalone registration, even if free-text notes mention an earlier computer. Notes remain escaped self-report and are never converted into links.

Reject consumer-supplied identity/origin/storage fields on both create and revise, including key presence with null/empty/array values. For parts, registration's explicit top-level allowlist is `_token`, `category`, `subtype`, `model`, optional `identifier_kind`, `identifier`, `photo`, `idempotency_key`, `answers`, `physical_damage`, `safety_concern`, `reason`. Revision's allowlist is `_token`, optional framework `_method`, `idempotency_key`, `expected_version`, `answers`, `physical_damage`, `safety_concern`, `reason`. Reject all other fields, including `data_status`, `intake_class`, `origin_kind`, `parent`, `parent_id`, `parent_item_id`, `parent_subject_id`, `parent_links`, `removed_from`, `detachment_event_id`, `batch_id`, `storage_id`, `storage_subject_id`, `storage_assets`, `embedded`, `quantity` and bulk arrays. Also add explicit parent/origin-field rejection to existing consumer device requests; no consumer registration is a processor provenance endpoint. Strict answer-key/value schemas reject nested relation payloads. No parent lookup is performed to validate a supplied reference; the error reveals no existence/ownership information.

On rejection, flash only safely typed category/subtype/model/observation fields and a valid command reference, plus expected version on revision. Exclude identifier/photo, framework tokens, protected/unknown fields and malformed nested values. The existing global `dontFlash` list alone does not cover new forged parent/storage fields; implement the scoped safe-input allowlist in the failure response rather than flashing the raw request. Link non-visible forged-field errors to the form heading, without echoing their values.

## 3. Observation and data contracts

New category/question schema version: **`PARTS-SELF-REPORT-1.0`**. Keep `SELF-REPORT-1.0` for every existing category and observation. Resolve the question version by category/subtype, store it on each new observation and reference it in the safe event payload. Freeze both schemas after use; render history using the observation's stored version, not a single mutable global constant. One physical part's immutable subtype selects its schema; observation revisions are append-only.

All listed answer keys are required, scalar, with no extra keys or N/A. `PASS` = **Worked when previously used**, `FAIL` = **Did not work when previously used**, `UNKNOWN` = **Unknown / not previously observed**. Declarative questions use `YES`, `NO`, `UNKNOWN`. These report existing knowledge; no current test is requested.

- **HDD:** `previous_operation` (PASS/FAIL/UNKNOWN: previously able to store/read data); `past_unusual_noise` (YES/NO/UNKNOWN: unusual noise already noticed during past use); `connector_damage` (YES/NO/UNKNOWN: already visible connector damage).
- **SSD:** `previous_operation` (PASS/FAIL/UNKNOWN: previously able to store/read data); `past_heat_issue` (YES/NO/UNKNOWN: overheating already noticed during past use); `connector_damage` (YES/NO/UNKNOWN: already visible connector damage).
- **Fan:** `fan_scope` (YES/NO/UNKNOWN: meets the bounded definition); `fan_electronics` (BASIC/ADDITIONAL/UNKNOWN, with the definitions in §1); `previous_rotation` (PASS/FAIL/UNKNOWN: rotation already observed during past use); `past_unusual_noise` (YES/NO/UNKNOWN: noise/vibration previously noticed).

Shared fields retain `physical_damage` and `safety_concern` (YES/NO/UNKNOWN), and required `reason` text up to 1,000 characters. Optional make/model remains max 150 characters and private JPEG/PNG photo remains max 5 MiB with existing content validation, re-encoding and compensation. Do not collect drive contents, interface scans, passwords, data samples, capacity, installation/removal dates or alleged parent identity. No automatic healthy/working answer is selected.

**HDD/SSD `items.data_status` is always `YES`**, on creation and every revision, including unknown condition, allegedly unused, empty, broken or consumer-erased drives. It means data-bearing capability requiring controlled treatment; sanitization never changes that capability to NO. The UI renders “Data-bearing: Yes — storage medium” as text, not an editable/hidden authoritative field. The server writes computed YES into the observation snapshot. A supplied `data_status`, especially NO/UNKNOWN, fails validation rather than clearing a gate. Database guards also reject direct non-YES drive writes. A consumer declaration is neither a sanitization attempt nor release/destruction evidence.

For fans, derive effective self-report `data_status=NO` only when current `fan_scope=YES` and `fan_electronics=BASIC`; otherwise use `UNKNOWN`. It is not a technician-confirmed absence of data. First registration rejects scope NO/UNKNOWN and electronics ADDITIONAL as above, but post-registration **revisions may record those newly learned answers**, retain the part's identity and display review guidance. They cannot conceal earlier versions or turn an existing hold into approval. No inspection/exception workflow is implemented in the first increment. Later services must treat unresolved scope/electronics and safety YES/UNKNOWN as review conditions, and separately guard any existing blocking exception. After actual receipt, the existing consumer edit restriction remains.

## 4. Settled storage facet and future parent linkage

First-increment `storage_assets` contains only:

- `subject_id uuid PRIMARY KEY REFERENCES subjects(id) ON DELETE RESTRICT`.
- `media_kind text NOT NULL CHECK (media_kind IN ('hdd','ssd'))`.

There is exactly one facet for every standalone HDD/SSD, sharing **`storage_assets.subject_id = items.subject_id = subjects.id`** and matching its subtype. There is no facet for a fan or existing whole-device row in this increment. No new UUID/public code, owner, ciphertext/hash, custody, observation aggregate or independent `data_status` is stored here. Storage data-bearing status is read from the canonical item, not maintained as a second projection. Parent/removal/batch columns are deliberately absent until the authorized processor workflow exists.

Use deferred PostgreSQL constraint triggers on `items` and `storage_assets` to require the correct facet exactly once at transaction commit, reject non-drive/wrong-kind/orphan facets, and reject a committed drive without a facet. PK/FK/CHECK constraints alone do not enforce the required reciprocal relationship. Ordinary facet deletion, subject reassignment or media-kind changes are forbidden by a persistence guard; controlled corrections require separately reviewed maintenance, as existing identity corrections do. Stage registration's facet inside its existing transaction before commit; failure rolls back all linked rows and private blobs.

**D3 dictionary revision:** `storage_assets` becomes a capability facet of a physical item, not a mutually exclusive `subjects.kind=storage` identity or a mandatory-parent child. This supersedes the baseline storage-kind/mandatory-parent/data-status-copy interpretation in §§3.5/4.4–4.6. The canonical physical drive is always `kind=item`, including future minimal embedded storage records. Future parent-linked media will have a canonical `items` projection and storage facet on the **same** subject, with a later server-only `processor_derived` intake class; never a second storage subject or comparison namespace. Before that later workflow is enabled, extend constraints and ownership/visibility/custody rules deliberately; no processor-derived insert is accepted now.

In that future workflow, nullable `parent_item_id` references the tracked parent, `embedded` describes current physical placement, and a removal event/batch reference records actual authorized detachment. Standalone origins remain parent-null/embedded-false; an asserted historical source does not fill those columns. Parent-linked origin is established by a processor with actor/facility/evidence, never by consumer form input. Already tracked media retains its UUID/EWC code, identifier comparison key, facet, treatment history and custody continuity when detached; untracked media gets one canonical identity when actually identified. Parent and child cannot be the same or form a cycle. Unknown physical matching requires review, not fabricated reconciliation. Do not copy a parent's serial to its drive or infer a serial-less match from a photograph.

D3's future BR09 duty for other embedded media (for example, phone/router storage) is preserved. Their processor-only media/category vocabulary and facet constraints must be extended in that later module before enabling those records; this first HDD/SSD enum neither admits other standalone parts nor removes the future embedded-storage data gate.

Detachment is a **deferred processor command** that rechecks parent/child scope, state, custody, versions, retry key and safe lineage, locks affected rows, records the removal and updates placement/custody atomically. It is not another consumer registration or first collection. No detachment endpoint, component inventory or returned-component lifecycle is in the first increment.

## 5. Migration and command compatibility

Use one new forward PostgreSQL migration when implementation is separately requested; this documentation increment creates/runs none. Preflight existing category/subtype rows and expected protections; stop on unexpected data with only safe counts/opaque references. Preserve the six original category IDs/definitions, subject UUIDs/codes, identifier ciphertext/HMAC/type, events, observation versions, commands, accounts and private files. Add/backfill only `items.intake_class=whole_device` for existing items. Do not fabricate old storage facets or parent history.

Extend the existing subtype CHECK and category validation trigger for the exact category/subtype pairs. Add intake-class vocabulary/correspondence and immutable-class protection while retaining every existing immutable field, crypto consistency check, identifier-kind guard and global unique index. The category-aware item guard must enforce HDD/SSD data YES on INSERT and UPDATE. Create the two-column facet and reciprocal/deletion guards described above. Use a writer-blocking migration transaction and stable insertion order; do not disable protection for normal registration or weaken any unrelated constraint. Destructive rollback remains refused; no database reset or key rotation.

**Fresh-install trap:** the original category migration imports mutable `Questions::CATEGORIES` and `Questions::VERSION`. Expanding the runtime list may make a fresh install seed `standalone_part` early under the old question version. Keep the old VERSION and six old categories unchanged, and leave historical migration files untouched. The forward migration must handle both the upgrade (new category absent) and fresh install (new row already seeded): insert when absent, or keep its ID and set only its intended label/support/question version when it has no associated items and matches the known seed shape. Unexpected contents block the migration. The old schema prevents part registration before this migration. Test both paths; do not blindly insert a second code or overwrite modified configuration.

Retain actor/action/key command uniqueness, eligibility/actor locking, expected-version rules and authorization of returned retry results. Successful same-command/same-payload retries resolve **before global duplicate checks**, returning the same device/facet/label with no added rows/photo. Changed payloads retain 409 conflict; a fresh matching command receives the exact identifier error. Preserve advisory comparison locks and catch only SQLSTATE 23505 naming `items_identifier_hmac_unique`; unrelated database failures propagate safely.

Do not inject computed `intake_class`, storage metadata, a default identifier type or normalized/forced data fields into **legacy validated payloads before command hashing**. Existing six-category command digest bytes must remain compatible, including omitted type and photo checksum behavior. New parts hash their validated submitted fields with the existing command context, then compute authoritative data/class/facet values separately. Observation/event question versions are resolved from frozen schemas; they are not client-controlled. Existing successful device retries and revisions must still work after the migration. Extend the normal atomic item/observation/event/custody/command/outbox/photo operation to include the facet, not a second aggregate/event sequence.

## 6. Downstream contracts, deferred from this increment

These specify how later modules recognize the approved vocabulary; they are neither an implementation schedule nor claims of available services.

- **Collection and compatibility (FR04/FR07–FR08; S04/S05/S06/S07):** treat standalone parts as eligible *candidate* items, not as whole computers. Require explicit active/reviewed acceptance for each `standalone_part` subtype at the point/facility/operator; a laptop or generic peripheral capability never implies drive/fan acceptance. Extend future `place_categories` with subtype-level capability configuration and limits before permitting these selections. Missing, synthetic or unverified real capabilities cannot advertise real acceptance. Apply geography, hazard/review and positive load estimates; small mass is not zero mass. No point, price, pickup promise or routing option is added now.
- **Inspection (FR19–FR21; S10):** frozen media/fan-specific technician schemas must replace laptop boot/display questions. Record media capabilities and hazards without copying stored data; record fan voltage/application/electronics scope and appropriate controlled function checks. Unknown capability/safety or out-of-scope discoveries lead to HOLD, with separate human approval. Current self-report PASS is not inspection or release permission. Final technician methods/rule fixtures belong to the later assessment module, not this registration increment.
- **Sanitization and release (FR22–FR23/FR26, BR09; S11):** attempts reference the drive's canonical storage/subject ID; keep method, tool/version, actor/time, evidence, verification and validation. Failed/unsupported/uncertain treatment and unresolved exceptions block release. HDD/SSD cannot use a consumer NO, generic N/A or formatting claim to bypass the gate. Destruction evidence or controlled surviving-storage records are required for applicable final closure. Choose appropriate methods in that later module; no default HDD technique is reused for SSDs.
- **Custody (FR16–FR18; S08/S09/S13):** one confirmed custody row per physical subject covers both item and storage facet. Parts use acknowledged item-specific receipt/transfer; registration is not physical collection. Future derived units inherit recorded facility custody through the controlled processor operation, without a duplicate consumer first receipt or facet handover.
- **Batch lineage/mass (FR24–FR29, BR10–BR12; S12/S14):** an independently received standalone part contributes its measured input kg once. A derived component removed from a parent already weighed into its originating batch is an output/lineage descendant, never an additional input to that same batch. Component mass and residual materials partition that input; do not add the parent's kg plus the child's kg. An output lot may group/allocate component mass but cannot assign a second physical identity to the drive; later lineage joins its canonical subject to the lot. Subsequent independently weighed processing at another stage is a separately labelled stage flow, not extra first-collection/recovery mass. Retain existing tolerance/variance and evidence rules; no new weighing UI or allocation module now.
- **Reports (FR31–FR32, BR13; S15):** distinguish whole-device, standalone HDD, standalone SSD, standalone fan and processor-derived cohorts, with explicit units/denominators. First-collection totals count unique physical subjects' acknowledged first receipt, not registrations, storage facets, handovers or detachment events. Facets never add a second collected/released unit. Reuse rate is released subjects / facility-received subjects within the same stated origin/subtype cohort/period; derived components are excluded from the original whole-device/standalone intake denominator and shown separately. kg comes from measured batch ledgers, not multiplying device counts by assumed weights; estimates and stage flows remain separate. Consumer lineage suppresses other owners/private identifiers; future exports retain spreadsheet-injection protection.

Real point/facility acceptance, safe local handling arrangements and partner operating parameters remain unverified. They must be established before offering real collection/treatment, not invented to finish project design. Loose batteries, arbitrary components, bulk inventory and resale remain excluded. Detailed derived workflows, technician procedures, component mass allocation and operational capability evidence are later-module work, not blockers for this bounded registration specification.

## 8. Acceptance tests for the first implementation

These are **specified tests, not executed results**. Retain the full existing authentication, identifier, upload, ownership and isolation suite. Run meaningful integration tests on guarded `ewaste_testing`; never write fixtures to development or bypass PostgreSQL isolation.

1. **PI01 Vocabulary/eligibility:** each exact part subtype succeeds; wrong/missing/cross-category subtype, battery, mains/industrial fan, unknown storage type, arbitrary/bulk/quantity payloads fail. Fan scope YES/basic and YES/unknown electronics succeed; scope NO/UNKNOWN or known additional electronics fail with useful associated errors. No unsupported record is created.
2. **PI02 Observations:** exact per-subtype keys and vocabularies accepted; missing/extra/nested keys, N/A, wrong results and malformed arrays rejected. All non-eligibility questions can be Unknown. New records/history use `PARTS-SELF-REPORT-1.0`; old observations retain/render `SELF-REPORT-1.0`. No power/disassembly/data-content requirement appears. Fan later revisions can honestly record lost eligibility/added electronics and retain all earlier answers plus review guidance.
3. **PI03 Data/facets:** HDD/SSD creation and revisions always project/snapshot YES; submitted NO/UNKNOWN/YES data overrides are rejected as server-owned. Exact single facet shares the UUID and subtype; no fan facet or second EWC/owner/custody exists. Fan computed NO versus UNKNOWN is correct. Direct SQL attempts to omit/mismatch/reassign/delete a facet, create a non-drive facet or change drive data to NO/UNKNOWN fail at the proper statement/commit boundary.
4. **PI04 Identity/retries:** encrypted serials, global cross-consumer/category/manufacturer and Serial/IMEI collisions, equivalent normalization, distinct unknowns and the exact non-disclosing field error. Parts reject explicit IMEI; old whole-device IMEI validation stays intact. Successful same-key/payload parts and pre-upgrade device retries return original IDs without extra facet/event/observation/custody/outbox/photo; changed payload yields conflict, new duplicate command fails. Exercise direct/competing PostgreSQL writes and the named-conflict mapping; unrelated database errors are not mislabeled.
5. **PI05 Origin/boundaries:** forged parent/removal/storage/origin/server-owned fields (including null, arrays and another owner's opaque reference) fail before parent lookup, with no matched-record disclosure or lineage changes. Ordinary consumer routes cannot derive/detach/promote storage, assign facility custody, release or create sanitization/batch records.
6. **PI06 Atomicity:** force failure before/after facet, event/custody/outbox and photo writes; assert no partial subject/item/facet/observation/event/command/custody/evidence/outbox and no leftover private blob. Rejections retain safe fields and valid retry reference, never identifier/photo/password/token content.
7. **PI07 Ownership/security:** guests redirect, staff remain denied pending later policies, other consumers receive private 404, suspended/stale sessions fail, CSRF and owned lookup/QR/print/photo remain protected. QR has only authorized opaque lookup; no identifiers/data contents/facet UUID is separately exposed.
8. **PI08 Migration compatibility:** upgrade populated six-category fixtures without changing their IDs/history/crypto/retry bytes; backfill class only; no invented old storage. Exercise absent and fresh-seeded new category paths, correct constraints/trigger restoration, safe unexpected-data abort and refused destructive rollback. Prove a migrated drive missing a facet cannot commit. Verify dedicated development/testing migrations at implementation time without resets.
9. **PI09 HTML feedback/views:** success redirects to owned detail with visible label-ready feedback; duplicate/scope/input/upload errors use existing single summary and field associations; safe values persist and private inputs are blank. Escaped notes/model; translated subtype labels and standalone origin in list/detail/history/print; no parent/removal, inspection, sanitization-complete or collection-accepted claims. Existing six-category forms and command behavior regressions remain covered.
10. **PI10 Manual browser acceptance:** desktop and 360px, keyboard-only selection/upload/summary links, actual focus and screen-reader announcements, unknown guidance, eligible/ambiguous/excluded fan cases, new parts plus old devices, retry/concurrent forms, private file reselection, print/scan/manual lookup. Assess contrast and wrapping visually. HTML assertions do not certify visual/accessibility acceptance; record actual results separately when implementation exists.

Later module conformance, **not first-increment implementation/tests or a schedule**: F01 explicit subtype point compatibility/unknown acceptance blocks real selection; F02 single-subject acknowledged custody and no receipt at detachment; F03 media-specific inspection/hold and required release gate; F04 failed/repeated/unsupported sanitization without consumer bypass; F05 derived identity reuse, parent/batch lineage and no doubled kg; F06 cohort/first-receipt/unit denominators, stage-mass separation and privacy-safe exports. No new downstream service is needed to implement PI01–PI10.
